Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before your first (or fiftieth) summer at camp.

About the Camp

What is Co-op Camp Sierra? +

Co-op Camp Sierra is a cooperative family camp that has been gathering in the Sierra Nevada every summer since 1938 — making 2027 our 89th year. The camp was originally connected to the co-op grocery store movement and was built on cooperative principles: campers of all backgrounds pitch in, share meals, lead activities, and look after one another.

It's a multigenerational community. We have campers in their second, third, and even fourth generation. New campers quickly feel the magic and keep coming back.

Where is camp located? +

Camp Sierra is located in the Sierra National Forest at nearly 5,000 feet elevation — midway between Huntington and Shaver Lakes, south of Yosemite. The address is 52000 Camp Sierra Road, Shaver Lake, CA 93664. It's about 90 minutes from Fresno and roughly 4 hours from the Bay Area.

Who comes to camp? Is it just for families? +

Everyone is genuinely welcome — families with young children, older kids, teenagers, adults in their 20s through 90s, couples, and single adults. Camp is multigenerational by nature and single adults find their footing just as easily as families. There's no typical camper.

When does camp run? +

Camp 2027 runs June 19–26, 2027 — our 90th year! Camp typically runs for one week in late June each summer. You can attend the full week or come for a shorter per-diem stay.

Lodging & Meals

Where do we sleep? +

Camp Sierra has several accommodation options at different price points:

  • Camping — tent sites in the camp grounds
  • Cabins (no restroom) — rustic cabins, shared bathroom nearby
  • Cabins (with restroom) — private cabin with bathroom
  • Lodge — lodge-style rooms
  • Duplex — larger unit suited for groups of 7+

All options include meals. See the Registration page for the full fee schedule.

Do we cook our own meals? +

No — meals are prepared by the Camp Sierra kitchen staff and are included in your registration fee. Breakfast, lunch, and dinner are served daily in the Dining Hall. A typical week features everything from scrambled eggs and biscuits & gravy to barrel-smoked tri-tip and baked chicken. Vegetarian options are available for registered vegetarian campers.

On All-Day Hike Day (Tuesday), everyone packs a bag lunch from the Dining Hall. Friday night is "Tip Night" — a tradition of showing appreciation to the kitchen staff who work incredibly hard all week.

Is the water safe to drink? +

Yes. Camp Sierra operates its own water and sewer system, overseen by a certified Water Master and monitored according to California state requirements. The water is tested regularly. Filtered water is also available at the tables in the Dining Hall and at the "Hydration Station" on the Store Porch.

Activities

What is there to do at camp? +

Quite a lot — see the full Activities page for the complete list. Camp staples include tie-dye, folk dancing, the Potholes swimming holes, the Talent Show, the Carnival, and the Live & Silent Auction. On top of those, campers organize their own activities each week at the Sunday planning meeting: recent years have included Zydeco lessons, herb walks, ukulele sessions, trivia, a challah bake, volleyball, board games, and more.

What are the Potholes? +

The Potholes are a series of massive granite pools fed by a rushing mountain creek, about a mile hike from camp. You can swim, slide, and float in cool Sierra water. It's one of the most beloved spots at camp — come prepared with a swimsuit and water shoes, and be aware of trail conditions (check the daily Twin Pines newsletter for updates).

Is there childcare? +

Yes! Co-op Camp childcare is available for children age 2 and up, Monday through Friday from 9 AM to 12 PM at the playground. On All-Day Hike Day (Thursday), childcare ends at 10:30 AM. Sign-ups happen Sunday at lunch (12:15 PM) at the playground. Parents are encouraged to volunteer for an hour or two during the week to help out.

Do I have to participate in everything? +

Not at all. Participation is always by choice. Many campers love being busy from morning to night; others prefer a slow week of coffee conversations, hammock time, and an evening dance. There is genuinely a pace here for everyone.

Cooperative Membership & Fees

What does "co-op" mean? Do I have to do anything? +

The cooperative spirit is at the heart of camp — campers pitch in to make the week happen together. If you register as a member, you contribute 5 hours of time toward camp administration or activities during the week and receive a discounted rate on your fees. It could be helping set up an activity, assisting in the kitchen, or staffing a carnival booth.

Non-members are completely welcome and pay the standard rate. Many first-time campers start as non-members and become members in later years.

How much does camp cost? +

Fees vary by accommodation type (camping, cabin, lodge, duplex), age, and member vs. non-member status. All fees include meals and lodging for the full week. There are also per-diem and day-use rates for shorter stays.

See the full fee schedule on the Registration page. A non-refundable $50 per-person deposit is included with payment.

Can I come for just a few days? +

Yes — per-diem rates are available for partial-week stays, covering meals and lodging for each day you attend. There are also day-use rates ($30/person/day) if you want to participate in activities without meals or overnight accommodations. See the Registration page for rates.

Practical Info

Is there WiFi at camp? +

There is a camp WiFi network — Camp Sierra Guest, no password required — though the signal generally reaches only the upper camp area and is strongest near the Store Porch. We'd encourage you to think twice before taking out your phone too often; part of the magic of camp is being present with the people around you.

What are the quiet hours? +

Camp enjoys quiet hours from 10 PM to 7 AM. This is a longstanding tradition and helps everyone — especially families with young children — get good rest between full days of activity.

Is smoking allowed at camp? +

The BBQ area and the flagpole fire pit are the only areas where smoking is permitted. No candles or open flames are allowed elsewhere — we're in a fire-sensitive national forest and take this seriously.

Is there a camp store? +

Yes! The Camp Sierra Store is open daily during camp week, typically 2–3 PM and 7:30–9 PM (with some variation — check the daily Twin Pines for hours). Ice cream is a beloved camp-store tradition.

Are there any wildlife concerns? +

We're in the Sierra Nevada, so yes — rattlesnakes have been spotted on the Potholes trail and around camp, and other wildlife is part of the setting. Keep your distance from any snakes you see; they generally want to avoid people. The Twin Pines newsletter will post any specific advisories during the week. Emergency procedures: if you hear a siren, please report to the flagpole.
